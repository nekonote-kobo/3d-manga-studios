    Manga Toon Effects NEW

    MTFX · Geometry Nodes › MTS FX …

    There are 54 effects in total, and all of them are built with Geometry Nodes. Their values appear in the object's Modifier, and they are also listed in the "Effect" section of the Sidebar, with lock and Keyframe buttons. By how they are built, they fall into the four groups below.

    1Screen linesFocus lines, speed lines, gloom lines (3 types) 2Manga line artEffect lines, manga symbols, drawn sound effects (21 types) 3Game-style VFXAttacks, skills, environment, warnings (25 types) 4Ink & brushBrush strokes, splatter, drips, wash (5 types)

    The categories in the Sidebar (effect lines, manga symbols, attacks, elements...) are sorted to make effects easy to find. The groups on this page are sorted by how the effects are built. Each group's layer diagram uses one representative effect as the example.

    Screen lines

    These are parented to the camera and drawn relative to the screen. They stay in the same place on screen when you move the camera, and the line width on screen does not change when you change the focal length.

    Manga line art

    Effect lines, manga symbols and drawn sound effects are black-and-white line art (material MTS FX Ink). They are placed in 3D space and always face the camera. Line and edge widths are a ratio of the screen height, so they look the same width even when they are far from the camera.

    Only drawn sound effects are text objects

    Drawn sound effects are added as a Blender text object and warped with Geometry Nodes (MTS FX Manga Text Warp). There is no face-camera setting; you set the direction with the object's normal rotation. See Feature Guide › Drawn sound effects for how to use them.

    Game-style VFX

    These work like particle effects in Unity or Unreal Engine: each effect is built by stacking several layers (flash, sparks, ring, smoke...). A layer is either a group of camera-facing cards, or a mesh such as a ring, arc or pillar. Time is controlled by the "Effect Progress" slider (0 to 1), which goes: anticipation → onset → peak → afterglow → fade out. Add Keyframes to it to animate the effect.

    Ink & brush

    Ink does not glow (normal semi-transparency, no additive blending). The default color is black, and ink does not fade out at the end, because it stays on the paper once drawn. The brush shape is made in the shader, with ragged edges, bristle streaks and dry-brush breaks (stronger toward the end of the stroke).
